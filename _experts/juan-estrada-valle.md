Juan Estrada-Valle is a six-time Chief of Party, thought leader, and executive with more than 25 years of experience managing economic growth programs in trade and investment, agri-food systems, value chain competitiveness, and U.S. foreign policy. He has a proven ability to navigate the technical, security, and political challenges inherent in global development in fragile states and highly political contexts. 

In his role as Vice President for Impact, Innovation, and Sustainability, Juan plays a senior leadership role responsible for advancing DAI’s mission through three interconnected areas: maximizing development impact, promoting and harnessing technical innovations, and ensuring the sustainability of global operations. As a member of the Office of the CEO, he coordinates across functions and business units to amplify DAI’s impact, scale disruptive approaches, harness data and technology, and ensure sustainable practices are embedded in all aspects of the organization. 

Before assuming his current role, Juan managed highly successful donor-funded projects in East and Southern Africa, Afghanistan, and the Balkans. Most recently, he managed the U.S. Agency for International Development (USAID) Africa Trade and Investment (ATI) activity, USAID’s flagship initiative under Prosper Africa, a U.S. Presidential-level national security initiative aimed at deepening strategic and economic ties between the United States and African nations, where he launched and oversaw private sector co-investments in 50 African countries. 

Juan had previously served as Vice President for Strategic Growth within DAI’s U.S. Government Business Unit, where he was responsible for increasing the technical and operational efficiency across DAI’s U.S. Government programs to maximize development impact and financial performance. 

Juan began his international development career as a Senior Scientist in trade economics at the International Crops Research Institute for the Semi-Arid Tropics in Southern Africa. He drew his inspiration to work in international development from his father, an internationally recognized development practitioner, and following a fortuitous 15-minute conversation with Secretary Henry Kissinger, in which he discouraged Juan—13 years old at the time—from pursuing a career in veterinary medicine saying, “We have enough people to care for our cats and dogs, what we need is people with the impetus to make a world a better place, so I urge you to reconsider.”  

* Ph.D. (ABD), international development, agri-food systems economics, Michigan State University
* Postgraduate Diploma, stabilization and development operations in fragile states, U.S. Naval Postgraduate School
* M.S., agricultural economics, Michigan State University
* B.S., agronomy, Universidad de San Carlos, Guatemala
