Dan Langfitt is an international development professional with experience at both the community and country levels, including more than a decade with DAI. He supports DAI's projects around the world, leads field research for new business opportunities, writes proposals, and develops training and communications products related to DAI’s economic growth, business and community resilience, economic and political stability, and private-sector engagement programming. Dan takes a systems approach to building resilient economies, improving rural livelihoods, managing natural resources, and developing public-private partnerships based on common interests. He specializes in applying market systems development and complexity science to build learning-driven teams, co-create and test innovations, and facilitate long-term change in complex economic systems, especially in fragile and emerging markets such as Haiti, northern Mozambique, and the eastern D.R. Congo. An experienced grants and subcontracts manager, he has designed and administered several adaptive private-sector engagement facilities for DAI. Dan also works for non-U.S. clients—he recently conducted a market systems assessment for the European Union in southern Madagascar—and is adjunct faculty at Columbia's School of International and Public Affairs, where he teaches enterprise development and value-chain analysis. Dan previously supported the U.S. Agency for International Development (USAID)'s Eastern Europe, West Africa, and worldwide programs in areas ranging from preparing for pandemic threats to catalyzing innovation and entrepreneurship.

In his most recent long-term role overseas on the [USAID Strengthening Livelihoods and Resilience Activity](https://www.dai.com/our-work/projects/democratic-republic-of-the-congo-drc-strengthening-livelihoods-and-resilience-slr-activity) in the eastern DRC, Dan served on its leadership team while managing his own portfolio of community and business partnerships, including one that [helped a seed company](https://dai-global-developments.com/articles/opening-the-taps-of-credit-lessons-from-a-credit-pilot-in-a-high-conflict-zone-in-eastern-drc/) expand its sales volumes to farmers in remote parts of an Ituri Province plagued by extremist militias. He supported a team of Congolese professionals learning to do development differently, and their efforts are helping Ituri emerge from cycles of crisis and humanitarian aid and discover [market-led solutions](https://www.marketlinks.org/blogs/bread-and-peace-and-honey-social-entrepreneurship-commercial-strategy) to become more stable, competitive, and resilient. Before moving to Ituri, Dan designed and managed the private-sector partnership facility of USAID’s flagship [market systems development project](https://www.dai.com/our-work/projects/mozambique-feed-the-future-agricultural-innovations-inova) in Mozambique, where his team helped national and international agribusinesses align their interests with the farmers that supplied them through tactics like supplier clubs that improved the quantity and quality of exports. Dan began his career in international development managing a community [agro-forestry and timber-marketing project](https://www.haitifriends.org/htrip) in Haiti’s Artibonite Valley. His working languages have included English, French, Portuguese, Russian, and Haitian Creole.

* B.A., international studies, Middlebury College
