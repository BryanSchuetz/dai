This month, the President of Mozambique, Daniel Francisco Chapo, formally launched eVerifica, a seed traceability system that will enable farmers to instantly verify the authenticity of seed before they purchase it. By enabling traceability from production to final user, the system will improve seed quality assurance, combat seed counterfeiters, boost farmer confidence in their investments, and ultimately increase agricultural productivity.

eVerifica is supported by [PROMAS (Promoting of Sustainable Agricultural Market Systems)](https://www.dai.com/our-work/projects/mozambique-promoting-sustainable-agricultural-market-system-promas), a market systems development project funded by the Swiss Agency for Development and Cooperation and implemented by DAI. The project’s goal is to increase income and employment opportunities for smallholder farmers and micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises in northern Mozambique.

![PROMAS seed traceability.jpeg](/uploads/PROMAS%20seed%20traceability.jpeg)

For smallholder farmers, adopting certified seed is one of the key drivers for improved productivity. Losses attributable to “fake” seeds are difficult to quantify precisely, but in 2020 the Alliance for a Green Revolution in Africa (AGRA) estimated that [20 to 40 percent of seed sold in Africa was fake](https://www.agra.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/AGRA-Framework-For-Seed-System-Investments-In-AGRA-Countries_v3-final-version.pdf), resulting in annual losses of approximately $400 million.

In Mozambique, PROMAS’s annual surveys of seed producers and distributors confirm that the prevalence of fake seed is one of the principal obstacles to growth in the seed sector. Farmers are less likely to invest in high-quality seed if they can’t be sure they’ll get the desired results; legitimate seed companies are less likely to invest in demand-creation activities in markets where counterfeit seed is common. Key stakeholders in the agricultural sector agree that a credible seed traceability system is urgently needed.    
To implement eVerifica, PROMAS partnered with AGRA, with Mozambique’s National Seed Authority (NSA), and with Quincewood, a technology company dedicated to building digital trust infrastructure that improves farmers’ access to authentic, high-quality agricultural inputs. The NSA hopes to replicate a similar model successfully implemented by AGRA and Quincewood in Tanzania. 

PROMAS is supporting the initiative by introducing the eVerifica system to key stakeholders in the Mozambique market, engaging the seed companies to understand the process, and running a national awareness campaign through TV and radio, with a particular focus on PROMAS districts and PROMAS-supported smallholders. 

This collaboration is the first partnership signed through the Promas Innovation Challenge Fund.  By the end of the pilot phase in August 2027, eVerifica will have been introduced to the market players and NSA staff will be fully trained in its use. Seed companies are already applying tacking cards to their bags for the upcoming planting season, and next year it will be mandatory for all seed companies to use the eVerifica system.
